Trust, built in.

A tool that reads your clipboard should be easy to check. These are properties of the code, not promises.

Open source, MIT

Read every line on GitHub, build it yourself and compare. The rules for spotting lookalikes are a public document with tests.

$ cargo test --workspace
ok 49 core, 17 lookalike cases, 8 network, 7 CLI

Local only

Recognition runs on your machine. The recognition engine contains no network code, and the build fails if anyone adds it.

$ cargo deny check bans
bans ok  reqwest, hyper, tokio, rustls: not allowed

Your clipboard stays yours

Kenning writes to it only when you click. Content that password managers mark as private is skipped before it is read.

New to address poisoning?

  1. You send to someone you know

    A friend, a client, your exchange. Their address now sits in your wallet history.

    0x28C6 c062 … 3bf2 1d60

  2. Someone sends you a tiny transfer

    It comes from an address generated to share the same first and last characters. That takes minutes on a graphics card.

    0x28C6 c0aa … AAaA 1d60

  3. You copy from your history

    Both addresses look right at a glance. Checking the first and last four characters no longer tells them apart.

    Two addresses, one look
